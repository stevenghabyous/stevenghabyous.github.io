Systematic FX trading research

Aug 2026 to present, six-person graduate team

In progress View on GitHub

Can simple trading rules make money on the euro-dollar exchange rate after trading costs? I wrote down 20 rules and the reason each should work before testing any of them, so I could not cherry-pick a winner afterward. The best one looked great on paper, but it did no better than you would expect from picking the luckiest of 20 random strategies.

Pre-registered search across 20 rules in 11 mechanism families on 10 years of EUR/USD. Walk-forward at 15-minute (18 windows) and 4-hour (10 windows) bars with a rolling training window, 1 pip round-trip cost, an untouched holdout from April 2024, and a Bonferroni bar of 0.0025. The top rule was benchmarked against 400 synthetic paths per candidate.

My part
Feature screening by rank IC, a VWAP study tested against a placebo level, and the deep dive on the TK cross (per-window, long vs. short, timeframe check), plus an MT5 Expert Advisor.
Finding
Trading costs, not clever signals, decided the results. Rules that traded more lost more, almost in perfect order.Losses ranked almost perfectly by trade frequency. A 1-pip round trip is about 20% of a typical 15-minute move but 4.7% of a 4-hour move, and noise alone was profitable in 49% to 55% of simulations.

The best rule, measured against luck

The best of the 20 rules, an Ichimoku TK cross, returned +22% out of sample. I ran the same rule on 400 random price paths with no real structure, and it beat 79% of them. Is that enough?

Optiver: Nasdaq closing auction

2025, Kaggle competition data

Small, real edge View on GitHub

Can order book data predict where a stock moves in the next 60 seconds of the closing auction? My first version looked better than it was, because it was partly tested on days the model had already seen. After fixing that and testing only on future days, the model beat a "predict no move" baseline by a small but honest margin.

LightGBM (MAE objective) on 5.2M rows, about 200 stocks over 481 days. Engineered spread, auction imbalance ratio, and order flow. Out-of-time split: first 70% of days for training, next 10% for early stopping only, final 20% scored once. Identifier columns removed after date_id ranked fifth in importance under a random split.

LSTM stock forecasting

2025

Lost to a naive baseline View on GitHub

A deep learning model predicted Tesla's next-day price with an R² of 0.97, which sounds excellent. But simply guessing "tomorrow's price equals today's" scored even higher. The model had mostly learned to copy yesterday's price, and it called the direction of the next move correctly only about half the time.

Two-layer LSTM on 60-day windows of TSLA closes, scaler fit on training data only, early stopping on the most recent 10% of training windows. Evaluated against a persistence forecast and directional accuracy over March 2024 to March 2025.

How I work

Four habits that show up in every project above.

Decide before you look

Write down the hypotheses and the test before seeing results, so the winner can't be cherry-picked. The 20 trading rules were frozen before any backtest ran.

Beat something simple

A model has to beat a naive guess to mean anything. The LSTM didn't, and saying so was the useful result.

Test on the future

Validate on time periods the model has never seen. Fixing a random split in the Optiver project turned a flattering number into an honest one.

Compare against luck

A profitable backtest is common by chance. The TK cross had to beat 400 random runs, and the best of 20 tries, before it counted.
